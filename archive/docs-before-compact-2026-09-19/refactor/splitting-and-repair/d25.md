### D25 — The pipeline is tested against the control, and that found a real bug

**2026-09-17.** `tests/tests/test_repair_pipeline.py`, 18 tests: the sequence run with real
tools on real defective meshes, asserting the **known correct answer** rather
than what the code does today. Distinct from `tests/tests/test_repairer.py`, which tests
structure with step 4 stubbed.

Every expected value comes from `sphere_correct` — 760 faces at **+4094.9** —
because each probe is that sphere with one defect injected. **Volume is signed
and compared against the control, never against the input**: a ratio to the
input is meaningless when the input is the broken thing (`doubles` halves,
`seam` gains, an inverted mesh changes sign).

Fixtures are committed to `tests/probes/` (492 KB) and regenerated by
`tools/make_probe_meshes.py` if missing. Two builders were added: `allbad`
(previously a scratch artefact surviving only as a file) and `inverted_third`.

#### The bug: a third inverted comes out inside-out

`sphere_inverted_third` — every third face reversed, scattered rather than a
contiguous cap — produces a **topologically flawless sphere that is
inside-out**: 760 faces, nm=0, open=0, volume **−4094.9**.

Every guard misses it, each for its own reason:

| guard | why it misses |
|---|---|
| step 2, `volume < 0` | total is **+1364.4** — positive, the reversed faces being a minority |
| step 4 per-part | one shell, so the part's volume *is* the whole mesh's |
| PyMeshFix | unifies the winding, then picks the **wrong direction** |

**Only the 1/3 case fails**, which is what makes it a real finding rather than
noise. Measured across inversion fractions, every one else recovers:

| reversed | input volume | after PyMeshFix |
|---|---|---|
| every 2nd | +0.0 | +4094.9 |
| **every 3rd** | +1364.4 | **−4094.9** |
| every 4th | +2047.4 | +4094.9 |
| every 5th | +2456.9 | +4094.9 |
| every 10th | +3275.9 | +4094.9 |

**The repair exists; the trigger is what is wrong.** `by_geometry` run
unguarded before PyMeshFix gives +4094.9 on every one of those rows.
`winding_seams` reports **722 seam edges in 0 closed loops**, so a seam-count
trigger would catch this where `volume < 0` cannot — and unlike `by_seams` it
needs no connected reversed region, which matters because these faces are
scattered and cannot be split out.

Recorded as `@unittest.expectedFailure` rather than deleted or weakened, so the
gap is visible in every test run.

#### The suite was not testing a quarter of the pipeline

Counted after the fact, and the answer was uncomfortable: of ten probes,
**the split never split**. Every fixture was one sphere, so `by_shells`
returned `(mesh,)` every time and the whole of step 4 collapsed to a single
call — the per-part loop, the **per-part orientation guard added the same
day**, and `merge`'s concatenation had never executed under test.

| step | fixtures exercising it, before |
|---|---|
| welder | 3 of 10 |
| orient (step 2) | 2 of 10 — only the wholly-inverted case |
| CLEAN | 10 of 10, unconditional |
| **split / per-part / merge** | **0 of 10** |

Worse, a test had been written that *documented* the hole
(`test_no_fixture_takes_more_than_one_part_except_doubles`) rather than filling
it. A green suite is not a tested pipeline.

Two fixtures close it, and the second one's shape was measured rather than
guessed:

- **`two_shells`** — two correct spheres. Asserts both survive the split, since
  unsplit PyMeshFix rebuilds one surface and discards the rest.
- **`shell_inverted`** — a large correct sphere beside a **half-radius**
  inverted one. The small sphere contributes an eighth of the volume, so the
  signed total stays **positive (+3583.0)**: step 2 correctly skips and only
  the per-part guard can see the inversion. Two *equal* spheres do not work —
  their volumes cancel to −0.0, step 2 fires on that, and the per-part guard is
  never reached.

`test_every_pipeline_step_is_exercised_by_some_fixture` now fails if any stage
loses its coverage. Verified by removing `shell_inverted` and confirming the
guard goes red.

#### Two corrections to what was recorded earlier today

**`sphere_seam` is repaired correctly** — +4094.9, matching the control.
Earlier in the session it was reported as "190.80% volume, never re-wound".
That was a misreading of `volume_kept`, which is the ratio to the *input*
(+4094.9 ÷ +2146.2); the output was right all along, and PyMeshFix re-winds the
cap exactly as the archive says. The lesson is the doc's own: a ratio needs its
denominator named.

**The Blender routing table stands.** It was briefly contradicted on the
strength of running the whole frozen `stl_batch_fix.blender` on a raw fixture —
which is the *old pipeline*, not step 4, and re-does doubles, T-junctions and
the normal vote on unprepared geometry. Given a prepared part, Blender
reproduces the recorded numbers exactly: allbad **840f at +4094.9** against
PyMeshFix's 836f at +4092.9, and `fin` at **760f, 100.00% volume, 1 vertex
lost** (the apex). The frozen script was not modified; that test is not
repeatable from this repo and its conclusion is withdrawn.

#### Two observations about the old script, for whenever it is retired

Measured inside the full-script run before that approach was abandoned:

- **its normal vote has nothing to vote on** — `agree: 801, disagree: 0`.
  `mesh_io.write` derives normals from the winding, so a stored normal can
  never disagree with it. The technique needs an exporter's *original*
  normals, which `mesh_io.load` discards ([mesh_io.py:409] reads bytes 12:48).
- **its `split_t_junctions` scans the wrong edge class** — `link_faces > 2`,
  i.e. non-manifold edges. A T-junction produces **open** edges, so it finds
  none on `sphere_allbad`. `welder` scans open edges.

Neither is a reason to change the frozen script. They are reasons not to port
those two steps into any Blender rung that is built later.
